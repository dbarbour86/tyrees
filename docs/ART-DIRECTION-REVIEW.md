# Book Moar — Art Direction & Visual Review

**Purpose:** Make each client website feel professionally art-directed and distinct, without compromising conversion, SEO, accessibility, or performance.

## Before coding
1. Confirm client identity, source-of-truth, business goals, and approved scope.
2. Inventory **authentic usable images** (owner/team, service work, trucks/equipment, buildings, location). Document ownership/licensing and what each image actually depicts. Flag missing imagery rather than inventing it.
3. Identify primary conversion action and strongest verified trust signals.
4. Propose a unique visual direction: type, palette, photographic style, section pacing, CTA treatment, mobile behavior, and 2–3 meaningful signature details.
5. Use reference sites only for **principles** such as human storytelling, contrast, and conversion clarity. Do not imitate distinctive layouts, artwork, brand motifs, copy, or imagery.

## Homepage art-direction checklist
- **Hero:** immediate offer, location/audience where relevant, verified trust, call and request-service CTA; compelling responsive imagery and readable contrast.
- **Services:** favor 3 well-composed image-led features or another brand-appropriate editorial presentation, with clear paths to all substantive service pages. Cards are allowed when they serve comprehension.
- **About/owner:** give genuine people and stories prominence when approved assets exist. Avoid burying the story among credential cards.
- **Trust:** display verified reviews/ratings and press without fabricated or repeated claims. Show fewer highlights on the homepage when this improves clarity, with links to more.
- **Rhythm:** intentionally vary section scale, image/text balance, and background treatments. Do not apply the same card grid to every section.
- **Contact:** one clear primary conversion route and non-obstructive secondary contact methods. Do not imply inactive text/chat/booking features are live.
- **Responsive:** inspect desktop and mobile screenshots; check image focal points, crop, readability, floating controls, layout overflow, and CTA visibility.
- **Quality:** verify performance, keyboard/focus behavior, reduced motion, accessible contrast, correct metadata/schema/canonicals, useful internal links, and form behavior.

## Guardrails
- Never invent or misrepresent review counts, ratings, testimonials, awards, media coverage, licensing, years of experience, service areas, or team photos.
- Never use a competitor's images, distinctive graphic shapes, branding, or copy.
- Never let a visual refresh silently delete routes, SEO value, lead functionality, or useful supporting content.
- Never modify live domain, DNS/email records, production deployment, or paid integrations without authorization.

## Review questions
1. Does the first screen communicate what the business does, why to trust it, and how to contact it?
2. Would this design still feel like this client if the logo were hidden?
3. Are photographs telling a true story rather than merely decorating cards?
4. Is there enough contrast and rhythm to avoid a long, repetitive scroll?
5. Is the mobile experience as intentional as the desktop?
6. Can we show verified facts, functioning CTAs, accessible UI, and valid SEO checks?

**Outcome:** present screenshots and an evidence-backed QA summary for human approval; do not claim conversion lift without measured results.
