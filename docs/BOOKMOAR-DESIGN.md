# Book Moar Design Rules

This file holds Book Moar's evolving design system, section patterns, and anti-patterns.

## Core Principles
- Every client site should feel intentionally designed for that business.
- Do not create interchangeable template sites.
- Use hierarchy, typography, whitespace, imagery, and contrast deliberately.
- Avoid generic AI/SaaS presentation patterns.
- Do not make every section a card grid.
- Prefer real proof and real business imagery whenever available.
- The process is standardized. The visual design is not.

## Homepage Content Discipline

Research completeness does not require homepage completeness.

The research process should discover as much useful information about the business as possible. The homepage should display only the information necessary to help a visitor understand the business, trust the business, choose the appropriate service or path, and take the primary conversion action.

Do not create a homepage section simply because research uncovered information about that subject.

For every proposed homepage section, ask:
1. Does this help the visitor understand what the business does?
2. Does this help the visitor decide whether the business is right for them?
3. Does this increase legitimate trust?
4. Does this remove an important objection or uncertainty?
5. Does this move the visitor toward the primary conversion action?

If the answer to all five is no, remove the content from the homepage or move it to an appropriate internal page.

### Progressive Disclosure
Prefer progressive disclosure over displaying all available information at once.

The homepage should summarize. Dedicated internal pages should explain.

Examples:
- Homepage services -> primary service categories.
- Services page -> complete service offering.
- Homepage team -> key people and decision-relevant specialties.
- Team/About page -> complete biographies.
- Homepage reviews -> a small selection of strong, verified social proof.
- Reviews/testimonials page -> additional reviews when appropriate.
- Homepage FAQ -> only critical conversion questions; otherwise omit or place elsewhere.

Do not duplicate an entire internal page on the homepage.

## Visual Storytelling Over Information Density
When authentic business imagery is available, use it to communicate atmosphere, people, craftsmanship, location, product, or experience instead of relying entirely on text and cards.

Avoid excessive:
- card grids
- feature boxes
- badges
- icon lists
- accordions
- repeated trust statements
- repeated CTA variants
- long service catalogs
- text-heavy sections

A website should not feel like a database of everything discovered during research.

## Section Budget
Treat homepage space as limited.

Every section must earn its position.

Prefer fewer, stronger sections with clear hierarchy over a long sequence of individually acceptable sections.

Do not interpret "comprehensive" as "long."

Do not add filler sections merely to make a website appear complete.

## Conversion Hierarchy
Every prospect website must establish:
- ONE primary conversion action
- optional secondary action(s) only when justified by the business
- a clear path toward that action throughout the page

Do not allow multiple CTAs to compete equally for attention.

Repeated instances of the primary CTA are acceptable when contextually appropriate, but avoid unnecessary CTA variations that create decision friction.

## Existing Capability vs. Proposed Enhancement
Never present a proposed Book Moar feature as an existing capability of the prospect's business.

Clearly distinguish between:
1. VERIFIED CURRENT CAPABILITY
2. PROPOSED REDESIGN / BOOK MOAR ENHANCEMENT

Examples include:
- online booking
- appointment requests
- automated follow-up
- instant estimates
- online payments
- customer portals
- live chat
- SMS
- AI answering
- CRM functionality

If the existing business does not currently provide the functionality, the speculative website may demonstrate the concept only when appropriate, but it must not make factual claims implying the business already provides it.

## Industry-Specific Decision Paths
Do not force every business into the same website structure.

Determine how customers actually choose and convert in this specific business.

Examples:
- Salon: Service -> Stylist -> Booking
- Home service: Problem -> Service -> Trust -> Estimate/Call
- Restaurant: Menu/Experience -> Location/Availability -> Reservation
- Professional service: Problem -> Expertise -> Trust -> Consultation

The research should determine the conversion journey.

The starter system should determine quality and process.

It should NOT determine a universal visual layout.

## Editorial Rule
Research aggressively. Publish selectively.
